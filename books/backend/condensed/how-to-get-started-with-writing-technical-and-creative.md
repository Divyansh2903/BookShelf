## Writing with no Experience

With little or no writing experience, do two things:

1. **Read** (a lot, and never stop). Books, articles, quotes, poems: read them all. Start on Medium and Substack, or even quotes on Pinterest. If you're into tech, read many tech articles and some decent beginner-level books (far better than doomscrolling). It improves vocabulary, pattern recognition, and speech, and reduces mental overstimulation, improving performance overall. The author has finished 11 books this year and calls it their best decision.
2. **Write** (however juvenile it feels). You learn by doing: code to learn coding, draw to learn drawing, write to learn writing. If you "can't form words in your mind", nobody expects an 800-page novel on the first try. Just as you wouldn't expect someone who lacks focus to lock in for 12 hours on day one, but would have them study in short intervals and gradually extend them, apply the same approach to writing:

- Start small, e.g. 2-3 quotes. Read more and apply what you learn (like a new word).
- Gradually increase length with short articles. Medium and Substack work well, from 2-min to 20/30-min reads.
- Keep improving by learning new writing patterns and words from other writers.
- Be consistent and do it for the love of the game.
- If your mind is blank, **start with journaling**. Write in a diary what you felt and did each day, fearlessly and honestly, as if no one will read it. Mental blocks fade and emotional well-being improves. No topic needed: write about your day, feelings, dreams, wins/losses, and ideas.

Every kind of writing has "formats", but ultimately you choose and create your own.

## Technical Writing

About knowledge and learning, not emotions. For a first technical article, don't agonize over "selecting a proper pattern". Pick a topic you know well, make notes, arrange them, and write readably and clearly. Nobody cares about impeccable vocabulary in technical writing.

- Pick a topic you are very confident about.
- Make your own rough notes.
- *"Many smart people don't want to actually help other people. They just want to show everyone how smart they are."* Don't do this. Make the article a) helpful to others and b) good for your own revision.
- Write without caring about a "universal format".

## Creative Writing

About emotions, feelings, and experiences rather than knowledge. Creative pursuits like writing and drawing improve how you "see" the world.

Example: writers often use the fall season as a metaphor for letting go of the old, withered parts of ourselves.

There are no rules. Write down whatever you see, hear, feel, or dream, without worrying about style or perfectionism.

If you have no topic, consider this verse from an old classic poem:

*Biting my truant pen, beating myself for spite,*
*"Fool," said my Muse to me, "look in thy heart, and write."*
*- From* **Loving in truth by Sir Philip Sidney**

You may draw on the external world, but creative writing ultimately has to come from inside.
